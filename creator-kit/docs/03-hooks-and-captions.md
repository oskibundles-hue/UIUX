# Hooks, captions and packaging

## What you are actually optimising for

Instagram's Reels ranking in 2026 leans on three signals, and they are not
equally weighted:

1. **Watch time** — total watch time and replay rate. The single biggest factor.
2. **Sends per reach** — DM shares. Weighted far above likes for reaching
   non-followers, because a send is a person spending social capital on you.
3. **Likes per reach** — still counted, now the weakest of the three.

Reach to non-followers works as an **audition**: your Reel goes to a small test
pool, Instagram watches early watch-time and send behaviour, and expands
distribution only if that pool responds. This is why the first 60–90 minutes
matter, and why the first second matters more than the other eighty-nine.

Practical consequence: **stop optimising for likes.** Optimise for "watched to
the end" and "sent to a friend". Those are different jobs and they need
different tools — retention editing for the first, caption CTAs for the second.

## Hooks

You have roughly **one second**. The hook is the visual, the on-screen text, and
the first spoken words working together — not just a caption slapped on top.

### The rule

- Text hook on screen from **frame one**, 3–7 words, large, high contrast.
- Motion in the first frame. Never open on a static shot or a title card.
- Never open with "Hey guys" or "So today we're…". You have spent your second.

### Templates that work for your content

**Curiosity gap** — state a fact that demands the rest of the video
- "This costs £40 and took 3 months"
- "Everyone here is in character. Including the bartender."
- "This is a normal Saturday for us"

**Stakes upfront** — tell them what could go wrong
- "If this shield breaks I'm out for the weekend"
- "One take. 200 people watching."

**POV / immersion** — the format your camera is built for
- "POV: you just walked into the wrong tavern"
- "POV: 6am, day two, everything hurts"

**Transformation** — the strongest format on the platform
- "From this → to this in 40 minutes"
- Open on the reveal, *then* cut back to the start

**Insider knowledge** — you know something the viewer doesn't
- "Three things nobody tells you before your first event"
- "What LARPers actually do when it rains"

**Direct address to a specific person**
- "If you've ever thought about trying this and chickened out — watch this"

**Number promise** — sets an expectation of length and structure
- "5 shots I get at every event"
- "Rating my kit, honestly"

### Your unfair advantage

LARP is **visually novel to almost everyone who sees it**. Most creators fight
for attention in saturated niches; you are showing people a world they have
never seen. Do not bury that. Lead with the most visually strange, beautiful or
impressive frame you have — armour, firelight, a hundred people in costume, a
shield wall — and let the curiosity do the work.

The corollary: **do not assume knowledge.** Most of your reach will be people
who have never heard the word LARP. Explain in passing, never condescendingly.
"Explaining this world to outsiders" is itself an infinite content pillar.

## Captions

### Structure

```
Line 1   Second hook. Visible before "…more". Must earn the tap.
Line 2   Blank.
Body     2–4 short lines. Context, story, or the thing the video
         couldn't say. Written like you talk.
CTA      One. Aimed at sends or comments, not likes.
Blank
Tags     3–5 relevant hashtags. Not 30.
```

### The CTA is the highest-leverage line you write

Sends are weighted enormously and they are the one signal you can *directly*
ask for. Ask specifically — a named person beats a generic "share this":

- "Send this to the friend who keeps saying they'll come next time"
- "Tag the person who'd survive about four minutes here"
- "Send this to someone who thinks this is just dressing up"

Comment-driving alternatives, when you want conversation instead:

- "Which character would you actually play? Be honest."
- "What's the one thing you'd want explained about this world?"
- Ask a question with a **low-effort answer**. "Sword or bow?" outperforms
  "what are your thoughts on immersive roleplay?" every time.

### Keywords matter now

Instagram search is a real ranking surface and it reads your caption. Write the
words people actually search: *LARP, live action roleplay, cosplay, medieval,
fantasy costume, larp armour, larp UK, foam weapons, immersive event*. Put them
in naturally-written sentences, not in a keyword pile.

### Hashtags

3–5, mixed by size. Thirty generic tags does nothing except look spammy.

- 1 broad: `#larp` `#cosplay`
- 2 mid: `#larpcostume` `#fantasycostume` `#larpuk`
- 1–2 narrow / community: your specific system, event, or region

## Packaging: covers and the grid

- **Choose the cover frame deliberately.** Never let Instagram pick. Pick the
  single most striking frame — usually costume, firelight, or a face in
  character.
- **Keep text out of the bottom third and the top-left corner.** The grid crops
  covers to a different aspect than the Reel, and the UI covers the edges.
- **Consistent grade = recognisable grid.** This is the real payoff of using the
  same LUTs every time. Someone should recognise your post from the thumbnail
  before they read your name.
- **Cover text is optional.** A stunning image often outperforms an image with a
  headline on it. Test both.

## Sound

- Trending audio still helps distribution, but **your own audio wins on
  original content**. Nat sound of a shield wall, boots, fire, crowd — that is
  content nobody else has.
- If you use trending audio, pick it in the first 24–48 hours of it trending.
  After that the boost is gone and you are just one of thousands.
- Duck music under dialogue. Music at full level over speech is a swipe.
