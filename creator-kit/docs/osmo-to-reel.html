Why not 4K. Instagram re-encodes everything and downscales anything above 1080 on the long edge. Uploading 4K hands their resampler a job your editor does better, and it usually comes back softer. Deliver a clean 1080×1920 and you keep control of the downscale and the sharpening.

The bottleneck is conversion, not reach

Four fifths of your views come from people who don’t follow you, which means Instagram is already pushing your work to strangers. They are watching and then leaving. Roughly 0.5% of the people who saw you followed you — and your single best-performing post earned two follows.

So more reach will not fix this. Doubling your views at this conversion rate buys you another 77 followers. The leak is between someone finishing a Reel and deciding you are worth following.

Where the leak is

  1. Three posts on the grid. Someone taps your profile and finds almost nothing. There is no body of work to scroll, no reason to believe the next post will be worth it. This is the single biggest fixable thing, and it costs nothing but volume. If you are archiving posts, stop — it actively removes the evidence that you are worth following.
  2. The bio doesn’t say what following gets them. “Reel creator” plus a philosophy quote describes how you feel, not what they receive. A visitor should know your subject, your promise and where you are inside two seconds.
  3. Supercar POV is the most replicated content on the platform. The car is the star, not you. People watch the Ferrari, feel the thing they came to feel, and swipe on — there is nothing to attach to. That is exactly what a 0.5% conversion rate looks like.
  4. Your reach is shallow. 175K Reel views from about 15K accounts means the same narrow pool is seeing you repeatedly rather than the audience widening. Combined with 14% of your audience in India and Indonesia — classic broad, low-intent supercar reach — that is distribution without direction.
You already have the fix, and you sent it to me

The garage walkthrough is worth more than any of the car footage. Nobody else has it. “Anti Stock Supercar Experience” being built from an empty building is a story — it has stakes, a beginning, and an outcome people will come back to check on. People follow stories; they only watch cars.

Supercar POV is your reach engine. The build is your follow engine. You need both, and right now you are only publishing the one that doesn’t convert.

Capture

Everything downstream is capped by what you record. These settings are chosen for footage that has to survive a 4K → 1080 downscale and a platform re-encode, while staying gradeable.

Why 4:3 rather than 16:9

A 9:16 crop out of 4K 4:3 leaves you roughly 1620×2880 — still half again larger than what you deliver, so the downscale adds sharpness instead of just discarding pixels. The same crop out of 4K 16:9 gives 1215×2160: usable, but you have spent your margin and you cannot reframe vertically at all.

Shoot 4K 9:16 natively instead when a clip is Reels-only and the framing is locked — maximum sharpness on the delivered frame, zero reframing latitude.

The three that matter most

1. Lock white balance. Auto WB drifting mid-shot is the most obvious amateur tell and it is genuinely hard to fix, because the shift is non-linear. Daylight 5600K · shade 7000K · tungsten 3200K · firelight and tavern scenes 2800–3200K. A WB change between shots reads as a cut; inside a shot it reads as a mistake.

2. Get ND filters. Holding 1/120 in daylight means cutting light. ND8 for overcast and golden hour, ND16 for normal daylight, ND32 for bright sun and snow. Without them the camera runs to 1/2000 and your fight scenes strobe. This is the single clearest line between “action cam footage” and “a film”.

3. Audio is half your retention. People swipe away from bad sound faster than bad picture. Wind noise reduction on, camera out of direct wind, and grab a few seconds of room tone at each location — it makes cuts invisible.

For cars and garages specifically

  • Rolling shots — 60fps, 1/120, RockSteady. Slow the best two seconds to 50%; leave the rest at speed.
  • Mixed garage lighting is the hard case: LED ceiling panels, daylight through the door, and dark walls all at once. Lock white balance to the dominant source and let the rest fall where it falls. Fixing it later is worse.
  • Paint and chrome blow out fast. Expose for the highlight on the bodywork, not the ambient room — a recovered shadow looks fine, a clipped highlight on a car is gone.
  • Talking to camera at arm’s length — use Dewarp, not Wide. Wide up close stretches your face, and you are the thing people are meant to attach to.
  • Deep skin tones need protecting. Most warm “cinematic” grades drag them orange and muddy. The looks in this kit pin skin hue so the warmth lifts the room instead of your face.
02

Convert

D-Log M is a transfer curve, not a look. This step is technical and has one correct answer.

Use DJI’s own conversion LUT — not a generated one

DJI does not publish the D-Log M transfer function. Any “D-Log M → Rec.709” LUT built from scratch by a third party, or by an AI, is guesswork — and a wrong conversion is worse than none, because it bakes in a colour error that every later adjustment amplifies. Download the official one free from dji.com/lut.

Two reliable ways to apply it on mobile:

  • DJI Mimo — import, apply the D-Log M → Rec.709 LUT, export. Most reliable mobile path, because Mimo knows the format natively.
  • CapCut — Filters → Import LUT. Dependable on CapCut Desktop; on mobile it depends on your app version. If you have no import option, convert in Mimo or use the slider recipes in stage 03.

Convert first, then grade. If you try to fix the flat look with contrast and saturation sliders instead, you will tear the image apart.

Built for Instagram’s encoder

Every look carries three compensations baked in: a small black floor lift so shadows band less after transcode, a guard on extreme reds (the first thing 4:2:0 chroma subsampling smears), and a highlight rolloff so skies compress instead of clipping.

04

Cut

Watch time is the number one ranking signal, and the average Reel is watched for about 8.5 seconds. Every rule here exists to get past that.

  • First frame is a hard cut into motion. No fades, no logo, no slow push-in.
  • Shot length 1.5–3 seconds for most of the edit. When a shot outlives its information, cut.
  • Cut on motion — mid-gesture, mid-step, mid-swing. Cuts on stillness feel like stops.
  • Pattern interrupt every 5–7 seconds: speed ramp, sound drop, angle change, text card.
  • Design the loop. Match your last frame to your first and the replay is seamless — and replays count as watch time. This is free reach.
  • Burn in captions. Most people watch muted first. Auto-caption, then fix the proper nouns — it will mangle car models and brand names every time.
Sound

Your own audio beats trending audio on original content — cold starts, revs, doors, tools, the room tone of your own garage is material nobody else has. If you do use a trending track, use it inside the first day or two; after that the boost is gone and you are one of thousands. Always duck music under dialogue.

Instagram settings — set once

  • Settings → Data usage and media quality → Upload at highest quality: ON
  • Data Saver: OFF
  • Upload on Wi-Fi. On a weak connection Instagram compresses harder to push the file through. This is real and very visible.

Covers and the grid

  • Choose the cover frame yourself. Never let Instagram pick. Take the most striking frame — usually costume, firelight, or a face in character.
  • Keep text out of the bottom third and top-left. The grid crops covers to a different aspect than the Reel, and the UI covers the edges.
  • A stunning image often beats an image with a headline on it. Test both.

06

Grow

A page grows when it does the same recognisable thing repeatedly, well enough that the algorithm can learn who to show it to. Random good posts don’t compound. Formats do.

What you are actually optimising for

Reels ranking leans on three signals, unequally weighted: watch time first, sends per reach second — DM shares count far above likes for reaching non-followers — and likes a distant third. Reach works as an audition: your Reel goes to a small test pool, and expands only if that pool watches and shares.

The practical consequence: stop optimising for likes. “Watched to the end” and “sent to a friend” are different jobs needing different tools — retention editing for the first, caption CTAs for the second.

Content pillars

Your unfair advantage

It is not the cars. Supercar POV footage is among the most replicated content on the platform — the audience is watching the car, not you, which is why it reaches and never converts.

Your advantage is that you are building something and filming it. An empty building becoming a supercar experience has stakes, a timeline, and an outcome people will come back to check on. Cars get you the reach. The build gets you the follow. Publish both.

Hooks — you have about one second

Curiosity gapThis building was completely empty 30 days agoEveryone thinks you need money to start this. You need three things.
Stakes upfrontRent on this place is due in six days and I have zero cars in itI put everything into this before I made a single dollar
POV / immersionPOV: you’re building a supercar rental from nothingPOV: your office is a garage full of Ferraris

Insider knowledgeWhat nobody tells you about renting out exotic carsWhat a McLaren actually costs to keep on the road

Text hook on screen from frame one, three to seven words, large and high contrast. Motion in the first frame. Never “Hey guys” — you have spent your second.

The caption CTA is your highest-leverage line

Sends are weighted enormously and they are the one signal you can directly ask for. Ask specifically — a named person beats a generic “share this”.

For sendsSend this to whoever you’d bring on the first driveTag the friend who says they’re buying one “next year”
For comments — keep the answer low-effortWhich one should be the first car in here?Ferrari or Lamborghini. One word.

First caption line is a second hook — it must earn the tap on “more”. Write searchable words naturally — exotic car rental, supercar experience, McLaren, Ferrari, car business — because Instagram search reads captions now. Three to five mixed-size hashtags, not thirty.

Working targets, not laws: 3-second retention above ~60%, average watch time above half the length, sends above ~1% of reach. Your own best post is the benchmark worth beating.

Cadence

  • 4–5 Reels a week. Below three you lose momentum; above seven they cannibalise each other’s audition window.
  • Never two Reels within a few hours — they compete for the same test pool.
  • Be present for the first 90 minutes. Reply to every comment properly, with more than one word. That activity is itself engagement.
  • Don’t delete underperformers. It doesn’t help, and you lose the data.

The 30 days

Week 1

Baseline and system

Lock the capture settings and LUT workflow. Four Reels, one per pillar — and fix the bio and grid before you post any of them. Don’t chase reach — you’re collecting data. Write down the five diagnostic numbers for each.

Week 2

Format testing

Five Reels across three series formats. Same grade, same caption structure, different formats. You’re looking for which format wins, not which topic.

Week 3

Double down

Take the two best formats and make three more of each. Vary the subject, keep the structure identical. This is where a format becomes a series.

Week 4

Scale what works

Five Reels, at least three in your proven format. Remake your single best post with a different hook — reposting a proven concept with a new opening is how every large account works.

By the end you should have a format you can execute without thinking about it. That’s the actual deliverable.

Don’t bother

Bought followers and engagement pods poison your audience signal, so Instagram shows your posts to people who don’t care and reach falls. Follow/unfollow does the same and gets accounts actioned. Thirty hashtags does nothing. Deleting underperformers does nothing. The only durable lever is making something people watch to the end and send to a friend.
