# Creative ideas from the footage

Read after watching the trial Reel (`GT3 in, SF90 out`). Everything here is
something the footage already supports; nothing needs a reshoot.

## What the trial Reel does, and why

| Beat | Time | What it is | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook card | 0.0–2.6s | `GT3 IN. SF90 OUT.` over the GT3 rolling in | Names the payoff in the first frame so a non-follower has a reason to stay for the Ferrari. Text is on screen at frame one; fading in wastes the only second that matters. |
| Plate beat | 6.4s | Punch-in on "that one says NATY" | The first laugh in the footage. A 12% zoom on the line makes it land without adding graphics. |
| Work section | 12–36s | Aston fender-liner install, wheels off, "gotta get this customer out" | The shop actually working is the trust content. Kept to 24s so the reveal arrives before the average viewer bails. |
| Reveal | 36.1s | Cut to the red SF90, slower 10% punch held 1.6s | The payoff the hook promised. The scale is smaller and slower than the plate beat so it reads as a reveal, not a joke. |
| Name drop | 41.4s | Punch-in on "his SF90" | Second mention of the car, the moment people rewind to. |
| End card | 48.6s | `Send this to your friend with the SF90` | Sends per reach is the ranking signal that reaches non-followers. A send is the one action you can ask for directly. |

Captions are word-level from the audio, in the measured style (Anton at
0.80 width, `#FDFDFD` base, `#FBD101` active word, centre 72.6% down frame).

## Things to try on the next cuts

1. **Two hook variants per Reel.** Trial Reels exist for testing. Post the
   same edit twice with different first-2s cards, a week apart:
   `GT3 IN. SF90 OUT.` versus `MY BOY JUST PULLED UP IN AN SF90`. Keep whichever
   holds more of the 3s retention.
2. **Chapter lower-thirds from the FD pack.** `lt_9x16_service_wheels.png`
   for 1.5s at the start of the wheels-off segment, `lt_9x16_service_service.png`
   at the liner install. They tell a muted viewer what the hands are doing
   without a caption line.
3. **One big word, once.** On "FIRE" at the SF90 reveal, one oversized Anton
   word centred behind the car for 0.6s, then gone. The HyperFrames caption
   doctrine calls this an *embed* and its rule is right: one per Reel, never
   two near each other. It is the one place his style can take an animation
   without becoming a different style.
4. **Plate callout.** A thin gold rectangle drawn around `NATY 9` for the
   length of the line. Cheap to add in Remotion (a positioned div keyed to the
   word timing) and it makes the joke readable with sound off.
5. **Cover frame.** Choose the SF90 reveal frame (36.5s) as the cover in the
   Edits app, not the first frame. The grid thumbnail is a second hook.
6. **Sound.** Keep his voice as the primary track. If Edits offers a trending
   audio, mix it under at −18 dB or lower; louder and the captions and the
   speech fight.
7. **Series framing.** Title future shop days the same way: `<car in>. <car out>.`
   A repeatable title is what makes a grid recognisable before anyone reads
   the handle.
