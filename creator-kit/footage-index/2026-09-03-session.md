## Edited: 23-44-19 -> detailing Reel

Brightest of the batch at YAVG 114 and the only detailing-focused material —
wheel, brake, gloved hands, microfiber. Visually distinct from the build-out
footage, which is why it was cut first.

Silence-cut at -22 dB / 0.25s: **91.1s -> 69.0s**, 26 segments, 2.7s average.
Delivered as 2160x3840 / 29.97fps / 10.0 Mbps / 84.3 MB.
The -26 dB setting that suited the walkthrough footage only removed 10% here
and left 6.9s shots — thresholds are per-clip, not per-camera. Graded with
`AK_NQ_Signature`, exported 4K 29.97fps.

### Two limits hit on this export

**Bitrate floor vs target size.** 75 MB across 69s works out to 8.7 Mbps,
below the 10 Mbps floor for 4K. autocut held the floor and came in at 84.3 MB
rather than starve the picture. To actually land 75 MB, cut to ~60s.

**Loudness lands at -15.3 LUFS, not -14.** Single-pass `loudnorm` measures and
corrects in one go, so it approximates. Within ~1.5 LU is fine — Instagram
normalises anyway — but a two-pass loudnorm would hit the target exactly if it
ever matters.

### Exposure varies clip to clip

Graded, this clip lands at YAVG ~75 against the reference's 46. That is
correct behaviour, not a fault: the LUT is a fixed curve, this scene is
genuinely brighter (work lights under a car), and forcing it down to 46 would
crush the underbody detail.

If every clip should hit the same brightness, that wants a per-clip match
rather than one shared LUT — `match_grade.py` run against the reference for
each clip, which produces a LUT tuned to that clip's own distribution.
