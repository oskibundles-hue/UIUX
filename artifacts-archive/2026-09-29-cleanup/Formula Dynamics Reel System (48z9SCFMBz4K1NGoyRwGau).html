01 — The reels

Seven ads, four priority services

R5 is the prospecting cut — the only one that explains who Formula Dynamics is before asking for anything. The rest are service-specific and suit retargeting. Together R1, R2, R6 and R7 cover all four priority services named in the brand kit: exhaust, tuning, wheels, body kits.

An eighth reel was added after this page

This record covers R1–R7. R8 — the annual service package at $3,999 — was built afterwards and is not documented here. It is the first of the series to put a price on screen, and it states both what the package includes and what it doesn't, at full size rather than in fine print.

Nothing on this page is superseded; it is simply one reel short. For the current set of eight, plus the eight picture-only masters, see the Ad Room and Download Everything.

Two claims still need your sign-off

R2 carries no horsepower figure at all — the dyno plot has unlabelled axes and shows the gain as a shaded area, because there is no verified dyno sheet to quote. And the service process lines ("road and dyno tested", the PPF coverage tiers) were written from the public bio; confirm they match what the shop actually does before spending behind them.

02 — Sound

Where the 44 sounds sit

Each dot is one sound, placed by its measured spectral centroid — the frequency its energy centres on. Impacts sit low so they carry weight through a phone speaker; clicks sit high so they cut through. The engine recordings are Formula Dynamics' own, harvested from the GT3 clip.

How it was sourced

  • 7 are yours. Pulled from the shop's own footage. The GT3 clip peaks at 94% of its energy below 250 Hz with almost no wind noise — the cleanest engine in the library. R1's valve-open moment now plays the real exhaust.
  • 11 are recorded, curated from Kenney's CC0 libraries. Most of their 753 files are game assets and wrong for this — but their mechanical recordings beat synthesis outright, so every switch, click and metal impact is real.
  • 26 are designed, synthesised from scratch. Kenney has no sub drops, long risers or drones, so the cinematic layer stays built rather than sampled.

Why three layers per sound

Cheap sound effects are one layer. Each designed sound here is three — a transient to give the ear something to lock onto, a body carrying the character, and a tail setting the size of the room.

Impacts add a sub sweep underneath. Phone speakers roll off below about 200 Hz, so that weight has to arrive through the body's harmonics rather than the fundamental — which is why the impacts cluster at the far left of the chart above.

03 — Frame

Composed for the Reels safe area

Instagram covers the edges of every reel with its own interface. The brand kit specifies the margins, and the right margin is three times the left — because the action rail lives there. Symmetric gutters put content under the buttons.

04 — Brand

Five colours, one stripe, one face

Transcribed from BRAND-SPEC.md, not inferred. Green and yellow are stripe colours only — never a headline, never a background.

#FE0F13 — Red. CTAs, key words, underlines.
#FFFFFF — White. Logo and headlines on dark footage.
#000000 — Black. The primary background.
#1DB14B — Green. Accent stripe only.
#FFDE00 — Yellow. Accent stripe only.

The stripe has five segments

Measured off the master artwork: red 36.7%, black 21.4%, white 19.4%, green 17.0%, yellow 5.5%. On a black ground the black segment reads as a gap — that is the artwork, not a fault. It appears under this page's masthead at true proportions.

Never stand it on end, and never simplify it back to four.

Type

Bebas Neue for headlines — uppercase by design, set with slight tracking. It is bundled with the kit under the OFL. Barlow Condensed carries supporting type; Inter carries body.

Neuropol X is the brand's accent face but is commercially licensed and not bundled, so it appears nowhere in these reels.

05 — Engine

Every frame is a function of time

Each reel is one HTML file with a frame(t) that rebuilds every style from t alone. The renderer seeks frame by frame and pipes PNGs into ffmpeg, so output is deterministic — a re-render produces an identical file, and a retime is a number change.

const S = { HOOK:[0.35,3.6], LAR:[4.0,8.4], SCH:[8.8,11.8], END:[12.2,15.0] };

# retime a beat by editing S; re-render one reel
node tools/render.js scenes/r1-exhaust.html out/FD-R1.mp4 30 1080 1920

# check a change without a full render
node tools/frames.js scenes/r1-exhaust.html build/kf.png "1.5,4.0,9.0,13.0"

# rebuild the sound pack — seeded, reproducible byte for byte
python3 tools/build_sfx.py

All seven reels sit between −4.1 and −5.1 dBFS peak with means within 2.1 dB of each other, so they are level-matched across an ad set — and there is headroom left to drop a licensed music bed underneath. Do not use Instagram's consumer music library on a paid ad; it is not cleared for that.
