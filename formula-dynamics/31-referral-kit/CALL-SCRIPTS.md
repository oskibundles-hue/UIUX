## How every call goes

The openers use the pattern that tested best in the research (`RESEARCH.md`, video 2). It
has three parts.

1. **Ask for half a minute**. Say who you are, admit the call is out of the blue, and ask
   for 30 seconds. They can say no.
2. **Ask one question about them**. The question is where their customers' cars go for
   work. It turns the call into a conversation, not a pitch.
3. **Let them stop you**. Say "tell me if this isn't your thing." A quick no is fine. It
   saves you both time.

Then make the ask, which is the same on every call:

> "If one of your customers ever needs service, repair or upgrades, would you send them to
> Formula Dynamics? They just put OMARIE in the booking message, so the shop knows it came
> through you and me."

Be plain about who you are. You are a local creator who films cars and sends people to a
shop you trust. You are not FD staff. If you earn a referral fee, say so
when they ask. Hiding it costs more trust than it saves.

**Give first**. Ask every partner who their best customer is. When you meet or film an owner
who fits, send that owner to the partner. This works best with detailers, wrap shops and
rental fleets. Track it in the Notes column. A partner you have sent business to will listen
to you.

**Before each call, spend 5 minutes on the business**. Look at their site and their
Instagram. Find one real thing to mention, such as a car on their lot or a recent post.

## Openers, one per partner type (about 20 seconds each)

Fill the brackets. Read each one out loud a few times, then say it in your own words.
A slightly rough delivery sounds more real than a smooth one.

### Exotic rental fleet

> "Hi, is this [name]? Hey [name], it's Omarie. I make car videos here in Vegas. I know
> I'm calling out of the blue. Can I take 30 seconds to say why, and you tell me if it's
> worth more? ... Thanks. Quick question first. When a renter asks where to get their own
> car looked at, do you have a shop you send them to?"

If they ask why: "I send owners to Formula Dynamics. They do service and
upgrades on exotics. I'm looking for a few places that would pass their name on."

### Dealership (used, exotic or independent; ask for the service or sales manager)

> "Hi, it's Omarie, I'm a car creator here in Vegas. I know this is out of the blue, so I'll
> be quick and you can tell me if it's not for you. When a buyer wants work you don't do,
> say an exhaust, a tune or wheels, where do they end up?"

Dealers think in volume and price. Don't ask a big dealer to send its own service work
elsewhere. The fit is the buyer who wants something the dealer doesn't offer.

### Detailer

> "Hey [name], it's Omarie. I film cars around Vegas and I've seen your work on [car or
> post]. I know you weren't expecting a call. Can I take half a minute? ... When a client's
> car comes in with something mechanical going on, a noise or a light, who do you tell them
> to see?"

Then give first: "And who's a good client for you? I meet owners every weekend who need a
detail."

### Wrap and PPF shop

> "Hi [name], Omarie here. I make car videos in Vegas, and I saw the [car] you did on
> Instagram. Got 30 seconds? Then you can tell me if it's worth more. ... When a client
> wants the car to sound or go as good as it looks after the wrap, where do you send them?"

### Car club or meet organiser

> "Hey [name], it's Omarie. I film at a lot of the meets here and I've been to [their meet].
> Quick one, and tell me if it's not your thing. Do your members have a shop they all use,
> or does everyone ask in the group chat?"

For a club, the ask is different. Ask if FD can be named as a shop members can try, with
the code. Never ask to post in their group without permission.

### Storage garage (climate-controlled or collector storage)

> "Hi, is this [name]? It's Omarie. I'm a car creator in Vegas, and I know I'm calling out
> of the blue. Can I take half a minute? ... When an owner comes to pick up a car that's
> sat for a while and it needs work, do you point them anywhere?"

### Hotel concierge desk (ask for the chief concierge, not the front desk)

> "Hi, it's Omarie. I'm a local car creator. I'll be quick, and tell me if this isn't
> something your desk handles. When a guest drives their own exotic in and needs
> something looked at while they're here, who do you call?"

Call between about 10 am and 3 pm, outside check-in and check-out. A concierge may ask what
they get. Use the answer under "Is there something in it for us?" below.

## Four objections and what to say

**1. "We already have a shop we use."**

> "Makes sense, most people do. I'm not asking you to switch. If someone ever needs
> something that shop doesn't do, or can't fit in, would you keep FD's name handy? I can
> drop a card off."

**2. "Is there something in it for us?"**

> "Fair question. The honest answer is it's not my call. Nate, the owner, decides that, and
> I can ask him. What I can do is send people your way too. Who's a good customer for you?"

Never offer a cut, a fee or a discount. Write the question down and pass it to Nate.

**3. "Send me an email."**

> "Sure. What's the best address? And so I send the right thing, is it the rental customers
> or your own cars you'd be thinking of?"

Send the follow-up email the same day. Book the next call date on the tracker.

**4. "Not interested."**

> "No problem, thanks for hearing me out. Do you want me to leave you off my list?"

If they say yes, mark the row "Not interested" and write "do not call" in Notes. Never call
that number again.

## Compliance (read before the first call)

- **Business calls only**. Call a business's listed business number. Don't cold call
  private owners. Reach them through the warm routes in `PLAN.md`.
- **Check the Do Not Call list for any number that may be a personal cell**. Small
  detailers and club organisers often list their own cell. Check those numbers against
  the National Do Not Call Registry before you call. Skip any number that is on it.
- **No AI or recorded voice on cold calls**. Since February 2024, the FCC treats an
  AI-made voice as an "artificial" voice under the phone law (TCPA). That needs prior
  consent, which a cold call never has. You dial, and you speak. That includes
  voicemails, so no voicemail-drop tools.
- **The AI callback is only for QR leads**. It is for people who scanned the card and gave
  consent on the form. It never calls a partner or a number from this list.
- **"Don't call again" ends it at once**. Mark the row "Not interested" and write "do not
  call" in Notes. Do the same for a "stop" reply to the email.
- **No promises**. Never promise prices, horsepower, results, turnaround times or
  discounts. Nate quotes those. If someone asks, say the shop will tell them.
- **No commission offers**. Whether a partner gets any share is Nate's call. Never offer
  one, hint at one or agree to one. Pass the question to Nate.
- **Be open about the fee**. If you earn a referral fee, say so when asked.
