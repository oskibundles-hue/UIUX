# Research: how to win referral partners by phone

Written 24 Nov 2026 for Omarie. The question is what a one-person referral partner in Las
Vegas can copy when calling businesses to send car owners to Formula Dynamics (FD).

**Method**. YouTube auto-captions were pulled for each video and read as text. Timestamps
are mm:ss from the captions and can be a few seconds off. Quotes are cut from auto-captions,
so small word errors are possible. No video was watched, so delivery and tone are not
checked.

**Claimed and proven**. Every video below is a single person's experience or a pitch. None
shows data a reader can check, such as call logs, a control group or conversion counts.
"Claimed" means the speaker says it. "Proven" means the video shows evidence. In this set,
almost nothing is proven.

**Fit**. Each video is judged against one person, no staff, no budget, calling Vegas
businesses about an exotic and performance car shop he doesn't own.

## Where the material is thin

- **No video covers this exact job**. None is about a third party referring customers to
  someone else's shop. All are about owners or sales reps growing their own business.
- **Nothing on exotic or performance car shops**. The two detailing videos are the closest.
  Neither covers partners who serve high-end owners, such as rental fleets, storage or
  concierge desks.
- **No numbers anyone can check**. The figures quoted ("half a million calls", "one in
  every 10", "60% of customers") come with no data. Treat them as one person's story.
- **Most videos sell something**. A dialer, a $497 course, scheduling software, a marketing
  agency. That shapes what they say.
- **Some advice breaks the rules here**. Voicemail-drop tools are recorded voice, which the
  FCC rules out on cold calls. "20% off" and "only one space left" promise a discount and
  invent scarcity. Omarie can do neither.

What would close the gap is Omarie's own numbers. The PARTNERS tab logs every call and
outcome. After about 40 calls, it shows which partner type and which opener work.

## The 5 tactics worth copying, ranked

1. **Permission opener, then one question about them**. Ask for 30 seconds. Then ask
   where their customers' cars go for work, and invite them to stop you. Source: Glencoco,
   video 2 (06:41 to 07:23). Aim the question at work the partner doesn't do, as body shops
   do for the dent repairer in video 8 (33:18, 38:49). It fits every partner type and costs
   nothing, though the only proof is the speakers' word.
2. **Give first, and ask who their best customer is**. Omarie meets owners every week and
   can send them to detailers, wrap shops and fleets. That gives a partner a reason to
   send people back. Sources: video 1 (16:47), video 4 (02:05). Proof: none shown, but it
   costs nothing to test.
3. **Map the partner types, then name three businesses in each**. List every kind of
   business that already serves the same owner, then fill each with three real names.
   Seven types give about 21 partners. Source: video 4 (05:34 to 08:20). It turns
   "call businesses" into a fixed list.
4. **Five minutes of prep and one specific detail**. Look at their site and Instagram first.
   Mention one real thing in the opener and the voicemail. Source: video 3 (09:05). Use an
   honest version of the "calling about" voicemail (07:45): name the topic, never a rival.
5. **Stick to one script, make the calls, and log every result**. Use the same opener
   for the first batch of calls instead of improvising. Change it only after the log shows
   what works. Sources: video 3 (03:16), video 7 (05:35, 09:32). It ranks last because it
   is a habit, not a line to say.
